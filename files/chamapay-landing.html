How the money moves

You think in shillings. Chamapay handles the rest.

Pay in with M-Pesa

Send your contribution the way you already send money. You get the usual M-Pesa prompt on your phone.

It's held as USDC

The group's balance sits in USDC on Base — a dollar-backed stablecoin — so it isn't quietly losing value while it waits.

Withdraw to M-Pesa

When it's your turn or your goal is met, the money comes back out to your M-Pesa in shillings.

USDC on Base
Why a stablecoin, in plain terms

Chamas often save for months. Holding that money as USDC keeps it pegged to the dollar instead of drifting, and Base settles transfers in seconds for a fraction of a cent. Every movement is recorded on-chain, so the group's ledger isn't something anyone can quietly edit.

Questions people ask

Do I need to understand crypto to use this?

No. You send and receive money with M-Pesa in shillings. USDC on Base is what Chamapay uses underneath to hold and settle the balance — you never have to touch it.

Who holds the chama's money between rounds?

The pool is held as USDC on Base against the group's chama, not in one member's personal account. Every contribution and payout is recorded, and all members see the same ledger.

Can someone contribute without joining Chamapay?

Yes. That's what pay links are for. They open the link, see what the money is for, and pay with M-Pesa. No sign-up.

What happens if someone misses a contribution?

The group sees it in the ledger and the member gets a reminder. Chamapay shows what's outstanding; how the group handles it stays the group's decision.

Is my balance affected by the shilling moving?

Your balance is held in USDC, which tracks the US dollar. When you withdraw, it converts back to shillings at the rate at that moment, which is shown to you before you confirm.

What can I use a pay link for?

Funerals, weddings, medical emergencies, school fees, helping a friend hit a goal — anything a group of people would normally pool money for.
